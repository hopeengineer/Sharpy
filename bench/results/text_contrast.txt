Text legibility — WCAG contrast on rendered frames, and tier 4's proposal compiler
Mac mini M4 (4P+6E, 16 GB), macOS 26.6.2, 2026-09-04.

The gap this closes
-------------------
Two legibility assertions already existed: text inside the title-safe area, and text on screen long
enough to read. Neither asks the question a viewer asks, which is whether the text can be read at
all. White text over a bright sky is inside the safe area, on screen for six seconds, and invisible.

Method, and its stated approximation
------------------------------------
WCAG 2.1 relative luminance and the (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05) ratio. WCAG is a web-text standard
rather than a video one, so this is cited as a CRAFT RULE with its source rather than as a platform
requirement — but its 4.5:1 and 3:1 thresholds are the only widely published measurable numbers for
the question, and a real standard beats an invented threshold. The assertion WARNS; blocking a
delivery on an approximation of a web standard would claim more confidence than the method carries.

Text and background are separated by PERCENTILE (10th and 90th) inside each OCR box rather than by
segmentation. Inside a tight text box the pixels are close to bimodal — glyph and ground — and
unlike a mean, percentiles are not dragged around by antialiasing at glyph edges. The known
weakness: for very thin glyphs or a loose box, fewer than 10% of pixels may be text, and the method
then understates contrast by measuring background against background.

Measured on the user's reel
---------------------------
    530 text lines in 6.6 s
    219 below 3.0:1 (WCAG AA, large text)
    worst 1.3:1 — "+6-" at 49.00 s

VALIDATED BY EYE, not just by running. The frame at 6.00 s was extracted and inspected: the flagged
"RESULT" at 2.6:1 is tiny grey label text on a light card and is genuinely hard to read, while the
large white "-32.5 DB" over a dark navy shirt in the same frame is NOT flagged. The measurement
agrees with what is visible, on both the positive and the negative.

41% failing is high, and it is not an artefact: this reel carries a lot of small grey label text
inside its graphic cards.

Tier 4 — the VLM as proposer
-----------------------------
The specification forbids the agent from being the source of a claim, but a model is the best thing
available for NOTICING — "that caption looks hard to read" is what a model is good at and a
threshold is not.

So a proposal can only cause a MEASUREMENT. `CheckCompiler` maps it to a deterministic check or
rejects it with a reason. Four names exist and each has a measurement behind it: textContrast,
textSafeArea, textDuration, subjectInFrame. Rejections are kept, because a model that keeps
proposing something unmeasurable is describing a gap in the checks — which is a roadmap, not a
fault, and is the "compile-rate residue report" M4 asks for.

Pinned by test:
  · naming a verdict does not make one — "thisEditIsBad", "approve" and "block" all fail to compile
  · a proposal without a Vision pass is REJECTED, not silently passed
  · a proposal about a moment with no text there is rejected, not a check that trivially passes
  · a proposal more than a second from any observation is rejected as too far to stand for it
  · the set of known kinds is asserted exactly, so a name cannot be added without a measurement
