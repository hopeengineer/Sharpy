Diarization on REAL multi-speaker audio with reference annotations
Mac mini M4 (4P+6E, 16 GB), macOS 26.6.2, 2026-09-04.

Why this exists
---------------
I swapped a measured component (sherpa-onnx pyannote-3.0 + eres2net) for SpeakerKit and justified
it on convenience. Challenged, I re-measured — on 200 s of `say` output spliced together — and
concluded SpeakerKit was a regression, then asked the user for a real recording.

Both halves of that were wrong. The material is freely available with ground truth, and the
"proven better" baseline had itself only ever been proven on the same synthetic splice.

Corpora (both CC BY 4.0, both with reference RTTMs, neither synthetic)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  VoxConverse dev   216 recordings, 20.30 h, 1-20 speakers each. YouTube/broadcast audio,
                    hand-verified diarization labels. This is the closest public corpus to
                    what Sharpy actually edits.
                    https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/data/voxconverse/
  AMI dev (subset)  6 meetings, 2.37 h, 4 speakers each, Mix-Headset condition. Spontaneous
                    overlapping conversation — the hard case.
                    RTTMs from BUTSpeechFIT/AMI-diarization-setup (only_words).

Scoring: pyannote.metrics, both conventions reported. DER(0.25, no overlap) is the forgiving
convention; DER(0, +overlap) counts everything and is always worse. Speaker count is scored
separately, because for an editor the count is what drives "cut the interviewer" and a diarizer
can post a decent DER while getting the count wrong.

THE FIRST RESULT: the old baseline does not survive contact with real audio
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sherpa-onnx at threshold 0.5 — the exact setting that found "2 of 2 speakers" on the synthetic
file and was recorded in bench/results/diar.txt as the measured baseline:

    AMI meeting        truth    sherpa-onnx @ 0.5 reports
    ES2011a              4              104
    ES2011b              4              116
    IB4001               4              180
    IS1008a              4               64
    IS1008b              4               86
    TS3004a              4              159
    DER 68.25% collar / 77.05% full. Speaker count exact: 0 of 6.

So "sherpa got 2 of 2" was never a property of sherpa. It was a property of 200 seconds of
`say` output. The component I called proven had been measured exactly as carelessly as the one
that replaced it.

HEAD TO HEAD, after giving sherpa every advantage
--------------------------------------------------
sherpa's threshold was swept 0.5-1.4 across four embedding models (eres2net, NeMo TitaNet-large,
WeSpeaker CAM++, 3D-Speaker CAM++ en). Best single-meeting result: TitaNet @ 1.10, DER 4.98%,
5 speakers found against 4 — better than SpeakerKit on that one file.

It does not survive the other five. Tuned on one meeting, evaluated on six:

  AMI dev, 6 meetings, 4 speakers each        DER(collar)  DER(full)  count exact  speed
    SpeakerKit           (DEFAULT, untuned)       7.66%      20.17%      4 / 6      472x RT
    sherpa TitaNet       @ 1.10 (tuned on AMI)   14.92%      24.02%      0 / 6       19x RT
    sherpa eres2net      @ 1.10 (tuned on AMI)   13.50%      23.13%      0 / 6       16x RT
    sherpa eres2net      @ 0.5  (old baseline)   68.25%      77.05%      0 / 6       16x RT

  VoxConverse dev, 216 files, 20.30 h         DER(collar)  DER(full)  count exact  speed
    SpeakerKit           (DEFAULT, untuned)       3.67%       8.98%    127 / 216    377x RT
    sherpa TitaNet       @ 1.10                   12.32%      15.67%     56 / 216     15x RT

SpeakerKit wins untuned against a tuned opponent, on both corpora, at 20-30x the speed.

On the full 20.30 h corpus the margin is 3.4x on DER (3.67% vs 12.32%) and 2.3x on counting
(58.8% vs 25.9%), with sherpa under-counting 111 files and over-counting 49 — it is wrong in both
directions, which is worse than a bias because there is no correction to apply. AMI had already
decided this on 2.37 h; the larger corpus did not change the answer, which is the outcome you want
from a held-out run and is worth recording precisely because it is unexciting.

The tuning trap, stated plainly
-------------------------------
TitaNet @ 1.10 scored 4.98% on IS1008a and 14.92% across the six. The optimum is a spike:
1.05 -> 8.08%, 1.10 -> 4.98%, 1.20 -> 13.65% on that one file. A threshold sitting on a spike
found by searching one file is not a setting, it is a coincidence. This is the same error as
tuning against my `say` splices, which is why no such correction was applied there either.

SpeakerKit's own threshold was swept on the same AMI material for fairness:
    0.50 -> 7.67%   0.60 -> 7.66%   0.70 -> 7.64%   0.80 -> 8.82%   0.90 -> 29.07%
Its default is already at the optimum, and the optimum is a plateau rather than a spike. Nothing
to tune, which is its own kind of evidence.

Where SpeakerKit is actually weak
---------------------------------
Speaker COUNT on VoxConverse: 127 of 216 exact (58.8%), skewed toward under-counting
(-1: 35 files, -2: 13, worse: 6; +1: 32, +2: 3). DER stays low because the dominant speakers are
right and the missed ones are usually brief. For an editor this matters: "cut every question from
the interviewer" is a count-dependent instruction. `numberOfSpeakers` remains exact and should be
passed whenever the count is known — that guidance survives, on much better evidence.

On AMI it is 4 of 6, missing one speaker on ES2011a and inventing one on IS1008b.

VERDICT
-------
The swap to SpeakerKit stands, now on evidence rather than convenience: better DER and better
counting than the alternative on 22.7 hours of real annotated audio, at 20-30x the throughput,
in-process on the Neural Engine with no Python. It is kept at its DEFAULT settings, because the
sweep says the default is already optimal and everything else is fitting.

The earlier claim in this repo that automatic counting is "NOT VALIDATED" was correct about the
evidence at the time and wrong about the conclusion it invited. It is now validated, against
real material, and the honest summary is: good DER, mediocre counting, pass the count when known.

Reproduce: bench/README.md, "Diarization on real corpora".


===========================================================================================
ROUND 2 (2026-09-04): two more candidates, because "it wins" is not "it is the best available"
===========================================================================================

SpeakerKit had beaten sherpa-onnx. That is one comparison, not a survey, and its weak axis —
speaker COUNT, 127/216 on VoxConverse — is the axis an editing agent leans on hardest. So two
more engines from FluidAudio (Apache-2.0, CoreML) were measured on exactly the same material:

  clustering   pyannote segmentation + embedding clustering. Same family as SpeakerKit, so a
               difference is implementation rather than architecture.
  sortformer   NVIDIA Sortformer, end-to-end, models speakers jointly and predicts OVERLAPPING
               speech directly instead of clustering embeddings after the fact.

AMI dev, 6 meetings, 4 speakers each      DER(collar)  DER(full)  count exact   speed
  SpeakerKit            (default)             7.66%      20.17%      4 / 6       472x RT
  sherpa eres2net       @1.10 tuned          13.50%      23.13%      0 / 6        16x RT
  FluidAudio clustering                      25.42%      34.37%      1 / 6       136x RT
  FluidAudio Sortformer                      51.49%      53.95%    * 6 / 6       347x RT

VoxConverse dev, 216 files, 20.30 h       DER(collar)  DER(full)  count exact   speed
  SpeakerKit            (default)             3.67%       8.98%    127 / 216     377x RT
  FluidAudio clustering                      20.61%      24.49%     69 / 205 †   136x RT
  FluidAudio Sortformer                      28.37%      30.31%     91 / 216     347x RT

* THE ASTERISK IS THE POINT. Sortformer scored a perfect 6/6 on speaker count, better than
  SpeakerKit and better than everything else measured. It is worthless.
  `OfflineSortformerConfig.numSpeakers` is `public let numSpeakers: Int = 4` — a compile-time
  constant. Every AMI meeting has exactly four speakers. The model was not counting; it was
  returning 4, and AMI happened to agree six times out of six.

  VoxConverse, where speaker counts run 1 to 20, settles it: 42.1% exact, under-counting on 111
  of 216 files, by as much as -16. A model that cannot represent a fifth speaker cannot diarize
  a five-person conversation, and no amount of tuning changes that.

  This is the same trap as the `say` splices and the 4.98% threshold spike, met for a third time
  in one session, and it was nearly reported as a win. What caught it was not suspicion of
  Sortformer — it was that 100% counting sat next to 51% DER, and those two numbers cannot both
  be true of a working diarizer. When a result is better than it has any right to be, the fixture
  is usually answering instead of the model.

† FluidAudio's clustering backend CRASHED after 205 of 216 files:
  "libc++abi: terminating due to uncaught exception ... Failed to allocate memory IOSurface
  object". Its score is over the 205 that completed and is therefore slightly favourable to it —
  it does not include whatever it would have done on the 11 files it died on. It loses by 5x
  regardless, so the missing files change nothing, but the number is not clean and should not be
  quoted as though it were.

A note on the scorer, since it was wrong in a way that mattered
--------------------------------------------------------------
`score_der.py` originally loaded RTTM with `ann[Segment(start, end)] = label`, which writes to a
default track — so two speakers talking over the same span silently overwrote each other, and the
scorer discarded exactly the speech that an overlap-aware model exists to find. Fixed to give
every line its own track id. It changed Sortformer's full DER by 0.02 points, so it was NOT the
explanation for its score, but a scorer that penalises the property one candidate is being tested
for is not a fair scorer whether or not it happens to change the verdict.

VERDICT after four engines
--------------------------
SpeakerKit stays, at its default settings, having now beaten sherpa-onnx (4 configurations across
4 embedding models), FluidAudio's clustering pipeline, and Sortformer, on 22.7 hours of real
annotated audio, on both DER conventions and on speaker counting, while being the fastest of the
four. Its weakness is unchanged and unfixed: 127/216 exact counts, skewed to under-counting. Pass
`--speakers N` when the count is known.
